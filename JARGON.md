# Jargon Buster

This file gives simple meanings for the technical words in this project. The README uses few of these words. This file has the accurate words for readers who want them.

**Agent**
Software that does actions for a person or for an organization. For example, an agent can send a message or approve a request.

**Audit**
A check by a person who examines the evidence. An audit finds out if the system obeys its rules.

**Audit bundle**
A package of the evidence that an auditor uses. Tier 2 must have an audit bundle.

**Binding safeguards**
Safeguards that the system must obey. A person or a default configuration cannot easily switch them off. The source lists "safeguard bypass" as a risk, where safeguards exist but the system overrides them by default.

**Control**
An action or a rule that makes a risk smaller. For example, a log of each decision is a control.

**Decision receipt**
A record that the system gives to a person after a decision about them. It tells the person the decision and the reason. The person can use it to make an appeal.

**Delegation token**
Proof that an agent has permission to do an action for a person or an organization. The source also tells you to make sure, at the time of the action, that the permission is still valid.

**DPI (Digital Public Infrastructure)**
The shared digital systems that a country gives to its people. Examples are digital identity, payments, and public records. The source kit is for AI that operates on these systems.

**Ecosystem governor**
The oversight body for a full group of organizations that use the same systems. It sets the rules for who can take part. Tier 3 goes to this body for review.

**Evidence**
Documents, records, and test results that show that a control works.

**Evidence bundle**
A set of evidence in one package. You make it before you release the system, or before a big change.

**Explanation artifacts**
Documents or messages that tell people the reasons for a decision in words that they can read. Tier 1 must have these.

**Formal appeal**
A procedure that lets a person contest a decision. Tier 3 must have a formal appeal.

**Governance**
The rules, roles, and checks that tell who can do what with a system, and who is responsible when something goes wrong.

**Human-in-loop confirmation**
A person must confirm a decision before it has an effect. Tier 3 must have this.

**Impact**
How bad the result of a risk is for people, from 1 (low) to 5 (high).

**Independent audit**
An audit by a person or a group that is not part of the team that operates the system. Tier 3 must have this.

**Likelihood**
How frequently a risk can occur, from 1 (low) to 5 (high).

**Logging**
A record of each decision and each error of the system. Tier 0 must have logging.

**Override logging**
A record of each time that a person changes or stops a decision of the system. Tier 1 must have this.

**Priority score**
Likelihood multiplied by impact. The score is between 1 and 25. The score tells you the risk tier.

**Risk register**
A list of risks. Each risk has an owner, a score, a review date, and the controls that make it smaller.

**Risk tier**
A level of risk, from Tier 0 (minimal) to Tier 3 (critical). The tier tells you which controls the system must have.

**Traceability**
The ability to follow one request through the system to its result. Tier 0 must have traceability.

**TRACE**
Trust, Risk, Architecture & Conformance Evaluation. The source project uses TRACE to evaluate a system and to find gaps in its governance.

**Vibe coder**
A person who makes software with an AI tool, and who writes little or no code with their own hands.
